About

I am an Associate Professor in the College of Media & Communication at Korea University. I earned my B.A. from Korea University, M.A. from Michigan State University, and Ph.D. in Communication Arts from the University of Wisconsin–Madison. Before joining Korea University, I was an Assistant Professor in the Department of Communication Studies at New Mexico State University.

My research examines how emerging media technologies affect individuals and society, with particular attention to social media, artificial intelligence, political communication, digital journalism, and media effects.

Research

I study how new media technologies influence people's attitudes and behaviors across domains including politics, science, entertainment, and sports. I am particularly interested in rethinking established media-effects theories in light of rapidly changing media environments.

Much of my work has focused on political communication, including misinformation, public opinion, political participation, political knowledge, and political polarization. More recently, I have expanded this research to examine how people perceive, adopt, and respond to artificial intelligence at both individual and societal levels.
